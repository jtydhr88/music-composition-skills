The companion full-inventory library to SKILL.md. Compiled, deduplicated and merged from:

- **【M#】** 《もっと！思いどおりに作曲ができる本》 (Ken Kawamura), Chapter 5 progression patterns No.1–81
- **【Z#】** 《できる ゼロからはじめる作曲 超入門》, Appendix 1 progressions ①–⑯ (plus glossary entries)
- **【P#】** 《プロの曲作りテクニック》, KOUGA's "simple chord progressions" feature (1-1 through 4-5, 25 patterns total)

When the same progression appears in multiple books, entries are merged into one with all sources listed. Entry format: `Degrees | Key=C letter example | Mood/Scene | Notes`.
Major-key section uses Key=C, minor-key section uses Key=Am. `X/Y` = slash chord (Y is the bass note); `·` = chord changes every 2 beats within one bar; `｜` = bar line (8-bar patterns).

## Appendix: quick reference of concepts used throughout this library

- **Cyclic / reverse-cyclic progressions (循環コード／逆循環)**: standard loops repeating over a fixed bar count; dominant function (D) at the end makes it flow back to the top, tonic function (T) at the end makes it resolve.
- **Substitute chords (代理コード)**: same-function chords are interchangeable — T: I/IIIm/VIm; SD: IV/IIm; D: V/VIIm(♭5). The story stays, the color changes.
- **Two-five (ツーファイブ, II–V)**: Dm–G (and Dm7–G7, D7–G7); followed by I it becomes the two-five-one. Minor version = IIm7(♭5)–V7.
- **Secondary dominant (セカンダリー・ドミナント)**: a dominant seventh making a dominant motion toward each diatonic chord: A7→Dm, B7→Em, C7→F, D7→G (= double dominant / ドッペルドミナント), E7→Am.
- **Ura-code (裏コード, tritone substitution / サブスティテュート / 裏ドミナント)**: the ♭II7 a tritone away from V7 (D♭7 in Key=C) substituting for the dominant.
- **Subdominant minor (サブドミナントマイナー)**: IVm (Fm) borrowed from the parallel minor, plus its family (♭VI△7, ♭VII7, IIm7♭5); the greatest single source of setsunasa.
- **Backdoor cadence (バックドアケーデンス)**: the "back door" resolution IVm7–♭VII7→I.
- **Passing diminished (パッシングディミニッシュ)**: passing diminished chords linking roots by semitone (#Idim, #IIdim, #IVdim, etc.).
- **Cliché (クリシェ)**: a line technique where the chord largely stays put while a single inner voice or the bass moves along a (chromatic) scale; the bass version is called the bass-line cliché (ベースラインクリシェ).
- **F/G type (IV(onV))**: the understated substitute for the dominant chord, effectively G7sus4(9); a city pop favorite.
- **Transposition**: memorize all progressions by degrees; major-key diatonic chords are always I·IIm·IIIm·IV·V·VIm·VIIm(♭5) — with the 12-key table they port to any key (e.g. Key=E: E–F#m–G#m–A–B–C#m–D#m(♭5)).
